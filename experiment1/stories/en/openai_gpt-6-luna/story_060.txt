### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs and lit the lamp.

Every night, the beam swept across the black water, searching for ships.

And every night, just before dawn, it found the same small boat.

The boat was always far beyond the reef. It never moved closer. A single figure stood at its bow, one hand raised—not waving, exactly, but waiting.

Mara told herself it was fog playing tricks. Her grandfather had kept the lighthouse before her, and he’d warned her that the sea could make a person see whatever they missed most.

Then, one winter night, the lamp went dark.

Mara hurried up the stairs with a box of matches. At the top, she found the lens turning by itself, though the mechanism had stopped. Its glass glimmered with a pale, moonlit glow.

Outside, the boat was nearer than ever.

The figure at its bow was a young man in a soaked coat. Mara knew that coat. She had watched it disappear beneath the waves twenty years ago, with her brother inside it.

He lifted his hand.

Mara pressed hers to the cold glass.

For a moment, neither of them moved. Then her brother smiled, the way he used to when he’d stolen the last biscuit and hoped she hadn’t noticed.

The lamp flared.

Its beam swept over the water, bright as sunrise. The little boat turned toward the open sea. Her brother lowered his hand and disappeared into the dawn.

When the light faded, the lighthouse mechanism began to tick again.

Mara stayed at the window until morning. Then she went downstairs, made tea, and opened the old biscuit tin.

For the first time in years, she laughed.
