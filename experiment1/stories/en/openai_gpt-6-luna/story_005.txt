### The Lighthouse Keeper’s Map

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs with a lantern in one hand and a cup of tea in the other. At the top, she lit the great lamp and watched its beam sweep across the sea.

Her grandfather had kept the lighthouse before her. He used to say the light didn’t guide ships home. It reminded them that home was still waiting.

Mara wasn’t sure she believed him.

One stormy evening, as rain rattled the windows, she found a map tucked beneath the lamp’s brass base. It showed the coastline, the harbor, and a tiny island that didn’t exist on any other map. Beside it, in her grandfather’s handwriting, were the words:

**For the one who gets lost.**

The next morning, the storm had passed. Mara rowed toward where the island should have been. For hours, she saw nothing but waves and gulls. Just as she turned back, a fog bank opened, revealing a small green island and a wooden dock.

A boy stood there, soaked and shivering.

“I’ve been trying to get home,” he said. “But every time I sail, I end up here.”

Mara looked at the map. The island was marked with a single star, and a dotted line led away from it—not toward the harbor, but farther north.

“Your home must be that way,” she said.

The boy shook his head. “I don’t know where that is anymore.”

Mara thought of her grandfather’s words. Then she offered him her hand.

“Come on,” she said. “We’ll look together.”

They spent the day following the dotted line along the coast. The map led them to a quiet inlet, where a small fishing village appeared between the cliffs. An old woman ran down to the water as soon as she saw the boy.

“My son!” she cried, wrapping him in her arms.

That night, Mara returned to the lighthouse. She placed the map beneath the brass base, where someone else might find it someday.

Then she lit the lamp.

Its beam crossed the dark sea, steady and bright. And for the first time, Mara understood what her grandfather meant: sometimes home was waiting in a place you couldn’t see yet.
