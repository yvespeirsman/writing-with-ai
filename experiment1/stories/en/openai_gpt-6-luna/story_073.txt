### The Lighthouse Keeper’s Last Map

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs with a lantern in one hand and a cup of tea in the other. The lantern was for the ships. The tea was for the ghosts.

At least, that was what her grandfather had told her.

“Heavy fog brings them out,” he’d said, back when he was still alive and still climbing the stairs himself. “They gather on the rocks and ask for directions.”

Mara had laughed. Her grandfather had laughed too, though his eyes had been serious.

Now he was gone, and the fog had arrived.

It rolled over the harbor after sunset, swallowing the boats, the beach, and finally the lighthouse windows. Mara lit the great lamp. Its beam turned slowly through the white.

On the third sweep, she saw someone standing on the rocks below.

Mara took the cup of tea and went down.

The stranger was a girl, soaked through and shivering. Her dress belonged to another century, and her bare feet were bleeding.

“Are you lost?” Mara asked.

The girl nodded. “I’ve been looking for the way home for a very long time.”

Mara glanced back at the lighthouse. The lamp was turning above them, bright as a star trapped in a jar.

“Where is home?”

The girl pointed out to sea.

Mara looked into the fog. She couldn’t see anything there—not a ship, not a shore, not even the horizon. But she remembered her grandfather’s maps, all those careful lines drawn in ink across empty blue.

“Home isn’t always where you started,” she said. “Sometimes it’s where someone is waiting.”

The girl held out her hand. The fog shifted.

Far out on the water, a ship’s bell rang once.

The girl smiled. Her bleeding feet left no marks as she walked toward the sound. When she reached the edge of the rocks, the sea opened—not like a door, but like a path remembered.

Then she was gone.

Mara returned to the lantern room. On the table lay a map she had never seen before. Its ink was still wet. A new line stretched from the lighthouse across the sea, ending at a small black dot.

Beside it, in her grandfather’s handwriting, were the words:

**For the ones who can’t find their way.**

Mara poured the cold tea into the sink and made a fresh cup.

Outside, the fog waited.
