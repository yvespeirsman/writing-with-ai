## The Library of Lost Things

At the end of Bellweather Pier stood a library with no books.

Its shelves held single gloves, chipped teacups, keys with no locks, and hundreds of small objects the sea had carried back to shore. A brass button. A child’s red boot. A photograph blurred almost white.

Every morning, Mara unlocked the library and dusted the shelves.

“People don’t come here for things,” her grandmother used to say. “They come to remember what they were looking for.”

Mara had never understood that. She was good at finding things. She could identify the owner of a lost scarf by the way it was mended, or tell which fisherman had dropped a coin by the dent in its edge. But memories were slippery. They changed shape whenever you tried to hold them.

Her grandmother had been gone for three years. On the day Mara was sorting a box of driftwood, she found a small blue marble tucked between two pieces.

She knew it at once.

When she was eight, she’d dropped that marble through a gap in the pier. She’d cried until her grandmother promised the sea would return it one day.

Mara turned it in her palm. Inside, a white spiral curled like a tiny storm.

That night, she dreamed of her grandmother standing at the water’s edge.

“You found it,” her grandmother said.

“I found it years too late.”

Her grandmother smiled. “Things don’t know what year it is.”

In the morning, Mara placed the marble on the front desk. By noon, a little boy came in holding his father’s hand. He stopped in front of it.

“That’s mine,” he said.

His father looked doubtful. “You’ve never seen that before.”

The boy picked it up. “I dreamed about it.”

Mara crouched beside him. “Do you know where you lost it?”

He shook his head. “I haven’t lost it yet.”

The father laughed nervously, but the boy’s fingers closed around the marble as if he were afraid the sea might take it back.

After they left, Mara stood alone among the shelves. For the first time, she wondered if the library was not a place where lost things ended up, but a place where they waited—patiently, without knowing for whom.

She went to the window. Beyond the pier, the water glittered beneath the afternoon sun.

“Anything else?” she asked it.

The sea rolled in, laid a small silver key at her feet, and rolled away.
