### The Lighthouse That Kept the Moon

Every night, the moon vanished behind the cliffs of Bellwether Bay.

Every night, old Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs to find it.

The villagers said she was foolish. The moon went where moons went. It did not need finding.

But Mara remembered a night, many years before, when the moon had fallen into the sea.

She had been a girl then, standing on the beach with her little brother, Tom. The tide was high, the wind was wild, and the moon shone so brightly on the water that Tom had reached for its reflection.

The next wave took him.

After that, Mara built the lighthouse. She kept its lamp burning through storms and summers, telling herself that if she watched carefully enough, she might see the moon return—and perhaps Tom with it.

One winter night, a terrible storm battered Bellwether Bay. The lighthouse shook. Rain lashed its windows. Mara climbed the stairs, one hand on the railing and the other clutching a lantern.

At the top, she found a boy sitting beside the lamp.

He was soaked through, with dark hair plastered to his forehead. He looked exactly as Tom had the night he disappeared—not a day older.

“Mara,” he said.

She dropped the lantern.

For a long moment, neither of them moved. Then Mara reached for him, afraid he might vanish if she blinked.

“You’re late,” she whispered.

“I know,” Tom said. “I got lost.”

Outside, the storm eased. Through the glass, the moon appeared above the cliffs, pale and whole.

Tom stood and looked toward it. “I can’t stay.”

Mara’s heart tightened. “I’ve kept the light on for you.”

“I know,” he said. “That’s how I found my way back.”

He kissed her forehead, just as he used to when she was the one afraid of the dark. Then he stepped into the moonlight.

Mara didn’t follow.

She watched him go, and for the first time, she understood: the lighthouse had never been meant to keep anyone from leaving. It was there to help them find their way home.

The next morning, the villagers found Mara asleep beside the lamp, smiling.

And from that night on, whenever the moon slipped behind the cliffs, she let it go.
