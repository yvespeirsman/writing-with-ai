At the edge of the village stood a house with a blue door. No one remembered who had painted it that color, but everyone knew the rule: if the door was open, you could come in.

One rainy evening, a girl named Mara found it ajar.

Inside, the house was empty except for a tiny brass bell on the floor. When Mara picked it up, it rang once.

Far away, someone rang back.

She rang again. This time the answer came from beneath the floorboards.

Mara knelt and pressed her ear to the wood. A voice whispered, “I’m lost.”

“Where are you?” she asked.

“Somewhere the stars can’t reach.”

Mara looked through the window. The storm had swallowed the sky, but she climbed onto a chair and opened the shutters anyway.

“Stars!” she called. “Someone needs you!”

For a moment, nothing happened. Then a single point of light appeared through the clouds. Another followed, then another, until the whole sky glittered bright as a field of lanterns.

Under the floorboards, something sighed with relief.

A small silver moth fluttered out from a crack. It landed on the bell, folded its wings, and said, “Thank you. I was trying to find the moon.”

“Why didn’t you just ask?” Mara said.

“I did,” said the moth. “But I’m very quiet.”

The moth flew out through the open window, following the stars home. Mara watched until it disappeared.

When she turned back, the blue door stood wide open.

After that, whenever someone in the village felt lost—or too quiet to ask for help—they found their way to the house at the edge of town.

And if the door was open, they came in.
