### The Lighthouse Keeper’s Map

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs and lit the lamp.

Every night, the beam swept over the same black sea.

And every night, just before midnight, someone knocked at the door.

Mara had lived alone on the island for seven years. The nearest village was a mile of water away, and no boat could safely reach the shore after dark. Still, the knock came—three soft taps, always at the same hour.

At first, she searched the beach. Then she checked the windows. Eventually, she stopped looking.

On the seventh anniversary of her arrival, the knock came again.

Mara opened the door.

No one stood there. But on the step lay a wet piece of paper, folded into a square. Inside was a hand-drawn map of the island. A red X marked the cliff behind the lighthouse.

She took a lantern and followed the path.

At the cliff’s edge, she found a small brass door set into the rock. It had no handle, only a keyhole. Mara had never seen it before, though she had walked this path hundreds of times.

The map had a note at the bottom:

**You left this for yourself.**

Mara reached into her pocket and found a key she didn’t recognize.

It turned.

Behind the brass door was a narrow room, dry and warm, with a desk, a chair, and a mirror. On the desk sat a book. Its pages were filled with Mara’s handwriting.

The first entry was dated seven years ago.

*The storm took the boat. I’m the only one who made it to shore.*

Mara stopped breathing.

She read on. The entries described the weeks after the wreck: the lighthouse, the fog, the nights she heard someone knocking. Each page ended with the same sentence.

*If you ever find this room, don’t let the light go out.*

From somewhere below, the island shuddered. The lamp above the cliffs flickered.

Mara ran back up the path and raced to the lantern room. The flame had nearly died. Beyond the glass, the sea was no longer empty. A ship was struggling in the storm, its mast bent, its lights fading.

She fed the lamp oil and turned the great lens.

The beam swept across the waves.

The ship turned toward shore.

At dawn, when the storm had passed, Mara returned to the brass door. She left a new map on the step outside the lighthouse.

At the bottom, she wrote:

**You’re going to need this someday.**
