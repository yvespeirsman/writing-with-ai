### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs and lit the lamp.

Every night, the beam swept across the sea, over the black waves and the empty horizon.

And every night, the lighthouse whispered a name.

“Jonah.”

Mara had never known anyone named Jonah. She had inherited the lighthouse from her grandfather, along with a brass key, a stack of tide charts, and a note that said:

*If it calls someone you know, answer.*

But Mara knew no Jonah. So she kept the name to herself.

One winter evening, a storm rolled in so quickly that the fishing boats barely had time to turn toward shore. Rain rattled the windows. Wind shook the tower.

Mara lit the lamp.

The beam swept out across the waves.

“Jonah,” whispered the lighthouse.

This time, Mara heard something beneath the name: a faint tapping, like knuckles against glass.

She hurried down to the lantern room and pressed her hand to the cold window.

Far out at sea, a small boat bobbed between the waves. A figure stood in it, waving a lantern.

Mara grabbed the signal mirror. She flashed it once, twice, three times—the warning for dangerous water. The boat turned toward shore.

Then the light went dark.

The lighthouse had never gone dark before.

Mara ran downstairs, but the brass key was already warm in her pocket. She found a narrow door behind the spiral staircase that she had never noticed. The key fit.

Beyond it was a room filled with thousands of little glass bottles. In each bottle, a name shimmered like a tiny star.

Mara found one marked JONAH.

When she opened it, the lighthouse groaned around her. A voice rose from the bottle—a boy’s voice, frightened and breathless.

“I’m here,” it said. “I can’t find the harbor.”

Mara understood. The lighthouse wasn’t calling the name of someone lost at sea.

It was remembering someone.

She carried the bottle up the stairs and held it beside the lamp. The name inside glowed bright enough to fill the room.

The light blazed back to life.

Outside, the boat swung toward the harbor, following the beam through the storm.

By dawn, the fisherman had made it safely to shore. He was old, soaked, and shivering—but alive.

When Mara asked his name, he smiled faintly.

“Jonah,” he said.

That night, the lighthouse whispered a new name.

Mara reached for another bottle.

And answered.
