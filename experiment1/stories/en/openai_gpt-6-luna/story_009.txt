### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse at Bracken Point flashed its beam across the sea.

Every night, the beam found nothing.

Mara had lived beneath it for eleven years, since her father disappeared in a storm. She was certain the lighthouse remembered him. Sometimes, when the wind was quiet, the old lamp turned with a faint, uneven click—click-click, pause, click—as though it were trying to say something.

On the night Mara turned eighteen, the light stopped.

The village went dark. Boats hurried toward shore, and people crowded into the lighthouse keeper’s cottage, asking what had happened.

Mara climbed the spiral stairs alone.

At the top, the lamp sat cold and still. Beside it lay a brass key she had never seen before. It was warm in her hand. When she touched it to the lamp’s iron base, a small door sprang open.

Inside was a folded note, yellow with age.

*Mara,* it read in her father’s familiar, crooked handwriting. *If the light goes out, follow it.*

She stared at the dark window. “Follow what?”

Then, far out at sea, a light blinked once.

Not the lighthouse. Something beyond it.

Mara ran downstairs, took a lantern, and launched her father’s old rowboat. The sea was black and restless, but the distant light blinked again, always just ahead.

She rowed until her arms burned.

At last, she reached a tiny island that wasn’t marked on any map. A man stood on the rocks, soaked through, with silver in his beard.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Then he said, “You grew up.”

Mara’s lantern shook in her hand. “You were gone for eleven years.”

“I know.”

“Why didn’t you come home?”

Her father looked back at the sea. “I tried. Every night. The storm trapped me here, and the only way to keep ships from striking the rocks was to guide them with the light.”

Mara glanced toward the mainland. The lighthouse beam had returned, sweeping over the water.

“Then come back now,” she said.

He stepped toward the boat. Behind him, the little island’s light faded, as if it had been waiting only for her to find him.

Together, they rowed home.

From that night on, the lighthouse flashed across the sea as before. But sometimes, when the wind was quiet, it clicked twice, paused, then clicked once.

And Mara always answered.
