### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse at Greyhook flashed its beam across the sea.

Every night, it missed one wave.

Mara noticed this because she lived in the keeper’s cottage and had nothing better to do than count. One, two, three—the light swept over the water, and the fourth wave always passed unseen, dark as a secret.

“Lighthouses don’t miss,” her grandfather said when she told him.

“But this one does.”

He looked toward the tower. “Then maybe it’s looking for something.”

That winter, a storm arrived from the north and stayed for three days. It tore the fishing boats from their moorings and flung rain against the windows like handfuls of gravel. On the third night, the lamp went out.

Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs with a box of matches and her grandfather’s old lantern. At the top, she found the great glass lens cold and dark. Beyond it, the sea was impossible to see.

Then the beam appeared.

Not from the lamp—from the water.

A white line stretched across the waves, pointing toward the rocks below. And there, caught between two black stones, was a small wooden boat. Someone inside raised a hand.

Mara ran down the stairs and out into the storm.

The boat held a boy about her age, soaked through and shivering. She dragged him up the path, while the sea grabbed at his heels.

At the cottage, her grandfather wrapped the boy in blankets.

“Where did you come from?” Mara asked.

The boy stared into the fire. “I don’t know. I’ve been looking for the shore for a long time.”

Mara glanced at her grandfather. He had gone pale.

The boy reached into his coat and placed a small brass key on the table.

Her grandfather picked it up with trembling fingers. “Your mother had one just like this.”

Mara looked from the key to the boy. Her mother had vanished at sea before Mara was born. The only story she’d ever been told was that the ocean had taken her.

But the boy’s eyes were the same gray-green as Mara’s.

“Who are you?” she whispered.

He shook his head. “I think I’m your brother.”

Outside, the storm began to quiet. The lighthouse lamp flickered back to life, sweeping its beam across the water.

One, two, three.

And for the first time, it found the fourth wave.
