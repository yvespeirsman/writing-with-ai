### The Lighthouse That Forgot the Sea

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs and lit the lamp.

Every night, the beam swept across the harbor, over the roofs of the sleeping town, and out toward the black water.

Then, one morning, Mara looked out and saw that the sea was gone.

Where it had always been lay a vast field of silver grass, bending in a wind she couldn’t feel. At the horizon, the sky met the land in a clean, impossible line.

Mara hurried down the lighthouse steps and into town.

“Where’s the sea?” she asked the baker.

He glanced up from his dough. “What sea?”

“The sea that was here yesterday.”

The baker laughed, but when he looked through his window, the laugh stopped.

By noon, the whole town had gathered at the shore. They stood where the docks ended, staring at grass that stretched away forever. Boats sat tilted in the mud, their hulls cracked and empty. Far off, something bright flickered between the silver stalks.

Mara recognized the light. It was her lighthouse beam, still turning though she had put out the lamp.

That night, she climbed the tower. The lamp was dark, but the beam continued to circle the empty horizon.

She opened the glass door and stepped outside.

The wind smelled of salt.

Mara walked into the silver grass. It parted around her boots like water. With every step, she heard a faint, distant rush—the hush of waves against stone.

At the place where the beam touched the ground, she found a small wooden boat. Inside lay a child, asleep beneath a blue blanket. A glass bottle rested beside her, holding a single note.

Mara unrolled it.

*We are not lost,* it said. *We are only waiting to be remembered.*

The child opened her eyes. “Is it morning?” she asked.

“Not yet,” Mara said. “But soon.”

She carried the child back toward town. Behind them, the grass bowed, and the salt wind grew stronger.

At dawn, the sea returned.

It came quietly at first, a blue line far beyond the docks. Then it rolled over the silver field, lifting the stranded boats and filling the harbor with the sound of home.

After that, Mara lit the lighthouse every night.

And sometimes, just before sunrise, she saw a small boat on the horizon, sailing toward somewhere no map had thought to name.
