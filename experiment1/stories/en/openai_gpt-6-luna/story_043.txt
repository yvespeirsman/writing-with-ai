### The Last Light on Bellweather Island

Every evening at seven, the lighthouse blinked twice.

The first flash warned ships away from the rocks. The second told the people on Bellweather Island that the day was over.

Mara had lived beneath that light for most of her life. She knew its rhythms better than she knew the tides. But on the night the lighthouse blinked only once, she was sure something was wrong.

She climbed the spiral stairs, carrying her father’s old lantern. At the top, the great lamp turned in its glass room, sweeping the dark sea. Its mechanism groaned. Then the light flared—once—and went out.

Below, the island vanished.

Mara hurried down and found a boy standing on the shore. He was soaked through, shivering, and clutching a wooden boat no bigger than a bathtub.

“Where did you come from?” she asked.

“Across the water,” he said.

“There’s nothing across the water but the mainland.”

The boy looked past her, toward the lighthouse. “My mother said to follow the second light.”

Mara glanced up. The lighthouse was dark.

For a moment, she thought of waking the island’s keeper—the oldest resident, who had taught her every bolt and stair. But he was gone now, buried on the hill beside the lighthouse. And the wind was rising.

So Mara took her father’s lantern and walked to the end of the pier. She lifted it high.

The small flame trembled in the salt wind.

Far out at sea, another light appeared.

The boy let out a breath. “That’s her.”

The distant light drifted closer, bobbing against the waves. A boat emerged around it, its sails ragged and its deck crowded with people. They had been lost in the fog, following a lighthouse that no longer shone.

Mara held her lantern higher. The boy waved both arms. Slowly, carefully, the boat found the harbor.

By dawn, the lighthouse was working again. Mara had repaired the broken gear with a piece of iron from the pier and a stubbornness she hadn’t known she possessed.

That evening, at seven, the light blinked twice.

The island listened.

And far out on the water, one small lantern blinked back.
