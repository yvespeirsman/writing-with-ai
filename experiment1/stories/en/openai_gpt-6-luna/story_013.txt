At 2:17 every morning, the lighthouse blinked twice.

Mara knew because she had lived beneath it for eleven years, and because the old clock in the kitchen had stopped at 2:17 the night her father disappeared.

The lighthouse was supposed to be empty. Its lamp had been replaced by an automatic beacon, and the keeper’s rooms were locked. Still, each night: two bright flashes, a pause, then darkness.

On the twelfth anniversary of her father’s disappearance, Mara climbed the spiral stairs with a lantern in one hand and the key she’d found sewn into his coat in the other.

At the top, the great glass lens turned silently. No one stood inside it. But on the fogged window, someone had written her name.

Then came two knocks from below.

Mara ran down. On the landing between floors, a door she had never noticed stood open. Beyond it was a narrow room, warm with lamplight. Her father sat at a desk, older than she remembered but unmistakably himself.

“I’ve been trying to get home,” he said.

Mara wanted to ask where he’d been. She wanted to shout. Instead, she looked at the clock on his desk.

It read 2:17.

“Why didn’t you come down?” she whispered.

“I couldn’t,” he said. “Not until you came looking.”

For a moment, the years between them seemed no bigger than the stairs. Then the lighthouse flashed twice, and the room began to fade.

Her father reached for her hand.

Mara took it.

In the morning, the village found the lighthouse door standing open and the kitchen clock ticking again. Mara was gone. But every night after, at 2:17, the lighthouse blinked twice—and somewhere out at sea, a small boat answered with a single light.
