### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse swept its beam across the sea.

Every night, the sea sent back a different answer.

On Mondays, it returned silver fish. On Tuesdays, it returned fog. On Wednesdays, it returned the moon, broken into a thousand pieces.

But on the first night of winter, the beam found a small boat drifting toward the rocks.

A girl stood in it, gripping the mast. She was too far away for the keeper to hear her, but the lighthouse heard everything. It had stood on that cliff for two hundred years. It knew the language of storms, ships, and lonely people.

So it shone brighter.

The girl turned toward the light. She steered, and the boat slid past the rocks into the harbor.

At dawn, she climbed the lighthouse stairs carrying a lantern with a cracked blue glass.

“My grandfather’s boat,” she said. “I thought I’d lost the way home.”

The keeper looked at the lantern. “Where did you get that?”

She smiled. “He said it belonged to the lighthouse.”

The old keeper took the lantern in trembling hands. He remembered its blue glass. He remembered the boy who had carried it away long ago—the keeper’s little brother, who had vanished at sea.

Outside, the lighthouse turned toward the brightening horizon.

For the first time in two hundred years, it had found its way home.
