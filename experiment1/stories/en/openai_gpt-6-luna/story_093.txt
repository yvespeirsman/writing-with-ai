### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse on Gull Point sent a beam across the sea.

Every night, the beam returned empty.

Mara had lived in the lighthouse since she was six, when her grandfather took her in. He taught her to polish the glass, wind the clockwork, and never ignore a bell—even if it rang in a storm.

“Especially in a storm,” he’d say.

After he died, Mara kept the light burning. She did it alone, though the village had begun to say the lighthouse was useless. The harbor had a new beacon now, bright and modern, mounted on a steel tower. Nobody needed Gull Point anymore.

Then, one winter night, the bell rang.

Mara sat up in bed.

The sound came again: one deep note, rolling through the stone beneath her feet.

She climbed the spiral stairs and reached the lantern room just as rain struck the windows. Beyond the glass, the sea was black. The new harbor beacon blinked steadily in the distance.

The old lighthouse beam swung over the water.

And stopped.

Far out, a tiny light blinked once.

Then twice.

Mara gripped the railing. “A ship.”

She grabbed the speaking trumpet and the signal flags, but the wind tore her words away. The ship’s light blinked again—weakly now, drifting toward the rocks.

Mara looked at the old lamp. Its flame was low. There was only one can of oil left, enough for a single night.

She thought of her grandfather’s voice: *Especially in a storm.*

Mara poured the oil into the lamp.

The beam brightened, swept the sea, and found the ship. For a moment, the two lights held each other across the darkness. Then the ship turned, following the beam toward the safe channel.

At dawn, a small fishing boat limped into the harbor. Its crew came ashore soaked and shivering, but alive.

The captain found Mara by the lighthouse door.

“We saw your light,” he said. “It led us home.”

Mara glanced up at the tower. The lamp had gone dark. But, just for a second, she thought she saw a warm glow behind the glass—like someone inside had struck a match.

Then the wind shifted, and the glow was gone.

That night, Mara climbed the stairs again.

The bell rang once.

And this time, she smiled.
