### The Lighthouse at the End of the Map

The map ended at a blue scribble.

Mara had found it folded inside her grandfather’s desk, beneath old receipts and a photograph of a woman she didn’t recognize. The paper was brittle, the ink faded, but the scribble was unmistakable: a stretch of sea, a tiny island, and a red X beside the words **LIGHTHOUSE—KEEPER NEEDED**.

Her grandfather had been a lighthouse keeper. He had also been dead for twelve years.

Mara took the map to the harbor.

“Nothing out there,” said the boatman, squinting at the blue scribble. “Just fog and rocks.”

“Then it’ll be easy to find.”

He laughed, but he took her anyway.

They sailed past the last buoy as evening settled over the water. The fog rose without warning, swallowing the boat, the horizon, and eventually the boatman himself. Mara was alone.

Then a light blinked ahead.

Once.

Twice.

A small island appeared beneath it, steep and black, with a lighthouse perched on its highest point. Mara climbed the winding path in the dark. At the top, she found the door unlocked and a brass key waiting on the table.

Beside it lay a note in her grandfather’s handwriting.

**The light doesn’t guide ships to shore. It guides them home.**

Mara looked through the lantern room windows. Far below, the fog shifted. Shapes moved inside it—ships with broken masts, boats crowded with shadowy figures, a white sail that looked, impossibly, like the one in her mother’s childhood photographs.

Then the lighthouse went dark.

Mara found the lamp’s old mechanism, stiff with rust. She turned the crank. It groaned once, then again. The great beam swept across the sea, and the fog began to loosen.

One by one, the ships changed direction.

At the very edge of the light, a woman stood on the deck of the white-sailed boat. She lifted her hand.

Mara lifted hers.

The woman smiled, and the ship sailed into the night.

By morning, the island was gone.

Mara woke on the harbor pier with the map folded beside her. The blue scribble had vanished. In its place, someone had drawn a small golden light.

And beneath it, in handwriting she knew as well as her own, were the words:

**Keeper found.**
