### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, Mara climbed the hill to light the old lighthouse.

The town below had stopped sending ships out years ago. The harbor was quiet, the nets were folded, and the lighthouse had become less a warning than a habit.

Still, Mara climbed.

One stormy evening, as she reached the lantern room, she found a small brass key on the floor. It was warm in her palm. When she touched it to the lighthouse’s ancient control panel, a hidden drawer clicked open.

Inside lay a letter.

*Dear Keeper,* it began. *If you’re reading this, the light has gone too long without a visitor.*

Mara looked out at the dark sea. Far beyond the rocks, a ship’s tiny lamps bobbed in the storm.

She turned the great lens. The lamp sputtered once, then blazed to life, sweeping a golden path across the waves.

The ship changed course.

Below, in the town, windows began to glow as people woke and hurried toward the harbor. Someone rang the old bell. Someone else started a fire.

Mara watched the ship slip safely between the rocks. Then she looked down at the letter again.

At the bottom, in a different hand, someone had added:

*The light remembers every way home.*

The next morning, the town began repairing the harbor. And every night after that, Mara climbed the hill—not because she had to, but because someone, somewhere, might still be trying to find their way back.
