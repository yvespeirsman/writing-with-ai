### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse swept its beam across the empty sea.

Mara was the last keeper. She lived alone on the rocky island with a kettle, a stack of old books, and a radio that mostly hissed. The village on the mainland had long ago built a harbor light of its own. No ships needed Mara’s lighthouse anymore.

Still, she climbed the spiral stairs each evening and lit the lamp.

“Someone might,” she told the dark.

One winter night, the beam caught something bobbing beyond the reef: a small wooden boat, its sail torn to ribbons. Mara ran down to the shore and found a boy inside, shivering beneath a red blanket.

He was perhaps ten years old. He had no luggage, no shoes, and no memory of how he’d reached the island.

“What’s your name?” Mara asked.

The boy looked at the lighthouse. “I don’t know.”

She brought him inside, warmed soup, and wrapped his feet in wool socks. For days he said nothing. Then, one evening, while Mara polished the great brass lamp, he asked, “Why do you keep lighting it if no one comes?”

Mara held the cloth still.

“My father kept it before me,” she said. “He used to say a light isn’t wasted just because you can’t see who finds it.”

The boy considered this. “Did he say anything else?”

“He said the sea remembers everything.”

That night, the boy climbed the stairs with her. At the top, he watched the lamp turn, sending its bright circle over the water.

The beam swept across the reef.

For one impossible instant, the sea answered.

Far beyond the rocks, another light blinked once. Then twice.

Mara grabbed the radio. A voice crackled through the static—a fishing captain, lost in the storm and running low on fuel. He’d been following the lighthouse beam for hours.

When the boat reached shore at dawn, the captain stepped onto the rocks and stared at the boy.

“My son,” he whispered.

The boy ran into his arms.

Mara watched them go, the red blanket trailing behind like a little flag. The captain promised to return in the spring, with fresh paint for the tower and a new lens for the lamp.

That evening, Mara climbed the stairs alone.

She lit the lighthouse.

Out beyond the reef, a small light blinked twice.
