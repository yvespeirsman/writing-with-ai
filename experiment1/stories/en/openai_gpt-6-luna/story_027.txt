### The Lighthouse That Blinked

Every night, the lighthouse on Greyhook Point flashed once toward the sea, once toward the town.

The townspeople said the keeper had gone mad. There hadn’t been a ship in those waters for thirty years, and the old man, Elias, lived alone with a brass lamp and a cat that disliked everyone.

Then, one winter evening, the town’s power failed.

Windows went dark. Phones died. Snow swallowed the roads. From the harbor came the low, terrible sound of a horn.

Elias climbed the lighthouse stairs. At the top, he lit the lamp by hand.

One flash toward the sea.

One toward the town.

Out beyond the rocks, a ship answered with three pale bursts of light.

Elias gripped the railing. “I knew you’d come back,” he whispered.

The ship turned toward the harbor.

Below, people poured from their houses, carrying lanterns and blankets. They watched the lights guide the vessel safely between the rocks, though no one could see a captain at its wheel.

When the ship reached shore, its passengers stepped onto the snow. They were soaked, shivering, and very much alive.

The last to disembark was a girl with a red scarf. She looked up at the lighthouse and waved.

Elias waved back.

By morning, the ship was gone. So was Elias.

But the lamp still flashed each night: once toward the sea, once toward the town.

And whenever a storm came, someone always saw a red scarf moving among the waves.
