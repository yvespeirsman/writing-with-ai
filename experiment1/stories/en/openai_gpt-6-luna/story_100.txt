### The Lighthouse Keeper’s Map

Every morning, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs with a cup of tea in one hand and a map in the other.

The map was old, its edges soft as cloth. It showed the island where she lived, the reefs beyond it, and a stretch of ocean that had never been explored. Her grandfather had drawn it decades ago, though the blank part of the sea had always bothered her.

“What’s out there?” she had asked him once.

“Whatever you’re willing to look for,” he’d said.

Now her grandfather was gone, and Mara kept the lighthouse alone. Each night, she lit the lamp. Each morning, she studied the map.

Then, one stormy evening, a ship appeared where no ship should be.

Its light blinked once, then vanished. Mara rushed to the lens and turned the beam toward the open water. Through rain and darkness, she spotted the ship pitching toward the reefs.

She knew the safe route by heart. But the ship was beyond it, in the blank part of the map.

Mara looked at the old paper. For the first time, she noticed a tiny mark near the edge: a penciled star, nearly rubbed away.

She grabbed her coat, a compass, and the small rowboat tied below the lighthouse.

The sea was furious. Waves slapped the boat and filled it with cold water. Twice, Mara thought of turning back. Twice, she saw the ship’s light blink ahead.

At last, she reached it.

The ship had struck a rock, but its crew was safe. Together, they followed the lighthouse beam home.

The next morning, Mara returned to the tower and unfolded the map. Where the sea had been blank, she drew a new line: a narrow passage between the reefs.

Then she added a star.

Not because she knew what was there.

Because now she did.
