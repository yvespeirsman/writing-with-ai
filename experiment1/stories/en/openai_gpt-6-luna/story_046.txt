### The Lantern Keeper

Every evening, Mara lit the lantern at the end of the harbor.

She had done it for forty-two years, since the winter her father disappeared at sea. The town said the lantern was too small to guide a ship through the fog, but Mara lit it anyway. A little light, she believed, was still a kind of promise.

One November night, the fog rolled in so thickly that the harbor vanished. Mara climbed the hill with her lantern, shielding its flame from the wind. At the top, she saw another light below: a warm square glowing in the window of the abandoned coastguard station.

No one had lived there for decades.

Mara went down the hill. The station door stood open. Inside, a boy sat beside the cold fireplace, soaked through and shivering.

“I was looking for the harbor,” he said.

Mara wrapped him in her coat. “You found it.”

He stared at her lantern. “Is that what brought me here?”

“It might have helped.”

The boy’s name was Eli. His family had been crossing the bay when their boat’s engine failed. They had drifted in the fog until Eli spotted Mara’s light and rowed toward it in a little dinghy. His parents were still out there.

Mara ran to the harbor and rang the storm bell. Soon, windows lit across town. Doors opened. Men and women hurried down to the docks, calling for boats and ropes.

By dawn, Eli’s parents were safe.

The townspeople repaired the old coastguard station and installed a brighter lantern on the hill. Mara kept the old one in her cottage, its glass cloudy and its handle warm from years of carrying.

And every evening, even after the new lantern was lit, she carried hers to the harbor too.

Just in case someone needed a little light.
