### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse blinked once for the living and once for the dead.

That was what Mara’s grandmother had told her, anyway.

“First flash,” she’d say, pointing toward the white tower on the headland. “For the ships still at sea. Second flash, for everyone who never made it home.”

Mara had believed her until she was twelve. After that, she decided it was just something old people said to make the dark feel less empty.

Then, one winter night, the lighthouse stopped.

Mara woke to the silence. Through her bedroom window, the headland was a black shape against a blacker sea. No sweeping beam. No flash for the living, no flash for the dead.

She pulled on her coat and ran.

The lighthouse door stood open. Inside, the keeper’s room was cold, and the great lamp above it sat dark. At its base lay a man she had never seen before, soaked through and shivering.

“Are you hurt?” Mara asked.

The stranger looked up. His hair was white as sea foam. “I’m looking for my sister.”

“Where is she?”

He pointed to the lantern room. “She’s been waiting there a long time.”

Mara climbed the spiral stairs. At the top, a woman stood beside the dark lamp, watching the sea. She wore a blue coat and had one hand pressed to the glass.

The old man reached her. Neither of them spoke. They simply took each other’s hands.

Mara understood then: the lighthouse hadn’t gone out. It had been waiting for them.

The woman turned to Mara. “Thank you,” she said, and smiled.

Then the two of them were gone.

A moment later, the lamp roared to life.

Its beam swept across the water, bright and steady. One flash for the ships still at sea.

Then, after a pause, another.

Mara watched until dawn. She didn’t think it was a story anymore.
