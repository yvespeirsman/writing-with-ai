Every evening, Mara climbed the hill to light the old lighthouse.

The town had stopped sending ships out years ago. The harbor was silted, the docks were empty, and the lighthouse had not guided anyone in a long time. Still, Mara climbed the hill at sunset with a tin of oil and a box of matches.

“Who are you lighting it for?” her brother asked one night.

Mara shrugged. “Someone who’s lost.”

He laughed, but he went home before the fog rolled in.

That night, the fog came thick as wool. It swallowed the town, the hill, even the lighthouse steps. Mara lit the lamp. Its beam turned slowly through the darkness.

Once. Twice.

On the third sweep, a bell rang from somewhere beyond the harbor.

Mara froze.

The town’s old sea bell had been removed decades ago. Everyone knew that. Yet it rang again—three slow notes, drifting through the fog.

She hurried down the hill, carrying her lantern. At the water’s edge, a small boat rocked against the stones. A boy stood inside it, shivering, with a red scarf tied around his wrist.

“I followed your light,” he said.

Mara recognized the scarf. It belonged to her brother, who had left town that morning to visit the city.

She took the boy’s hand and led him home. Behind them, the lighthouse kept turning, its beam sweeping the empty sea.

The next evening, Mara climbed the hill again.

This time, when her brother asked who the light was for, he didn’t laugh.

“Someone who’s lost,” she said.

And together, they lit the lamp.
