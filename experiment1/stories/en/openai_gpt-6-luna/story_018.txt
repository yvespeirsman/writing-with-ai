### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse swept its white eye across the sea.

Every night, the sea answered with darkness.

Mara had lived in the lighthouse since she was small enough to sleep in a drawer. Her father had taught her how to polish the lens, trim the wick, and listen for ships in the wind. When he died, she kept the light burning alone.

“Ships don’t need to see us,” the villagers said. “The harbor’s empty.”

But Mara kept the lamp lit.

One winter night, a storm rose so quickly that the bell in the harbor began ringing by itself. Waves climbed the rocks. Rain struck the windows like handfuls of gravel.

Then, through the storm, Mara heard a knock.

Not at the door.

From inside the lighthouse lamp.

She climbed the narrow stairs and found a girl standing in the glass room, soaked to the skin and shivering. The girl wore a blue coat and carried a little wooden boat.

“Are you lost?” Mara asked.

The girl shook her head. “I’m looking for my brother. He went out fishing this morning.”

Mara looked through the glass. The sea was black and furious. The lamp turned, and its beam crossed the waves.

For one bright moment, a tiny boat appeared between two walls of water.

Mara grabbed the speaking horn. “Hold your course! Follow the light!”

The boat turned.

The girl pressed her wooden boat to the window. “He’ll hear you,” she whispered.

And somehow, above the storm, a voice called back.

By dawn, the fisherman had reached shore. The girl was gone. On the floor of the lamp room lay only her little wooden boat, still wet with seawater.

Mara placed it on the windowsill.

After that, people began coming to the lighthouse with their lost things: a sailor’s cap, a child’s scarf, a letter never sent. They said the light helped them find what they’d been looking for.

Mara never asked how.

Every night, she turned the lamp toward the sea.

And every night, somewhere in the darkness, something answered.
