### The Lighthouse at the End of the Map

The lighthouse appeared on no map.

Mara found it by accident, following a road that ended at the sea and then, impossibly, continued across the water in a narrow strip of pale stone. She had been driving for hours, trying not to think about the letter in her coat pocket.

The road led to a small island. On it stood a lighthouse, white once, now weathered gray. Its lamp was dark.

Mara climbed the stairs. At the top, she found a man asleep beside the lantern room, wrapped in a yellow blanket.

“You’re late,” he said, opening one eye.

“I don’t know you.”

“No,” he said. “But your mother did.”

The words struck harder than the wind.

Mara took the letter from her pocket. Her mother had written it in the hospital, in a shaky hand:

*If you ever find the lighthouse, ask the keeper what the light is for.*

The old man stood and brushed dust from his sleeves. “Your mother came here when she was young. She asked me the same question.”

“And what did you tell her?”

“That it was for people who were lost.”

Mara looked out at the sea. “That sounds simple.”

“It is. Most important things are.”

He led her down to the lamp. It was an enormous glass eye, clouded with salt. A brass lever stood beside it, polished bright where hands had touched it.

“Does it still work?” she asked.

The keeper shrugged. “When someone needs it.”

Mara almost laughed. “And who decides that?”

“You do, tonight.”

The sea beyond the windows had gone black. Far away, a storm gathered—a dark wall swallowing the horizon.

Then Mara saw a small light below. A boat, pitching in the waves, its lantern blinking in and out.

She didn’t hesitate. She pulled the lever.

The lighthouse burst into brightness.

Its beam swept across the water, and the little boat turned toward the island. Mara watched it crawl through the storm until, at last, it reached the sheltered cove below.

The keeper nodded. “There.”

“Who was on it?”

“Someone who needed a way home.”

Mara turned to ask another question, but the old man had vanished. Only the yellow blanket remained, folded neatly by the stairs.

At dawn, she stepped outside. The road across the sea was gone. Behind her, the lighthouse stood solid and ordinary on its island.

She opened her mother’s letter again. On the back, in handwriting she hadn’t noticed before, were six more words:

*When you’re ready, keep the light.*

Mara looked out at the water. Somewhere beyond the horizon, someone might be lost.

So she stayed.
