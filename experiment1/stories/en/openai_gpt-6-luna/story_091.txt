### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse flashed across the bay.

Every morning, it forgot.

The keeper, Mara, had learned not to mind. She told the lighthouse the date, the weather, and the names of the ships that passed. By dawn, it had forgotten all of them. Still, she kept talking.

“Storm coming,” she’d say, polishing the great glass lens. “The *June Bell* is late. And I found a blue button on the stairs.”

The lighthouse listened in its slow, stone way. At night, it sent its beam sweeping over the water, warning sailors away from the rocks.

One winter evening, a boy arrived at the door, soaked through and shivering.

“Have you seen my mother’s boat?” he asked. “It was supposed to come home before dark.”

Mara scanned the sea. Nothing but waves.

“Come inside,” she said. “We’ll wait together.”

The boy’s name was Eli. He sat by the stove and stared out the window while Mara told the lighthouse about him.

“His mother is missing,” she said. “Her boat is called the *June Bell*.”

The lighthouse turned its beam over the bay. Once. Twice. Again.

Hours passed. The storm grew louder. Eli fell asleep in a chair, his hands curled around a chipped mug.

Near midnight, the lighthouse’s beam stopped moving.

Mara rushed up the spiral stairs. At the top, she found the great lens pointed toward a narrow gap between the rocks—an opening no sailor would choose in a storm.

Out beyond it, a lantern flickered.

Mara rang the warning bell until her arms ached. The *June Bell* turned toward the light, slipped through the gap, and entered the bay.

At dawn, Eli’s mother carried him home.

Mara stood alone in the lighthouse, watching the sunlight wash the windows.

“Do you remember?” she asked.

The lighthouse hummed softly.

That night, when Mara climbed the stairs, she found a blue button resting beside the lens. She had lost it years ago. She smiled and tucked it into her pocket.

Then the beam swept out across the water—steady as a promise.

And, for the first time, when morning came, the lighthouse remembered.
