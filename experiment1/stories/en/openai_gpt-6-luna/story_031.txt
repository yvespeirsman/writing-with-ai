### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse at the edge of town swept its beam across the sea.

Every night, Mara climbed its spiral stairs and lit the lamp.

And every night, just before midnight, the lighthouse whispered her name.

“Mara.”

She never told anyone. The lighthouse had stood on the cliff for a hundred years, and people already had enough stories about it. Besides, she was the only one who heard the voice.

One stormy evening, the whisper came again.

“Mara.”

This time, the light went out.

She ran to the top. Rain slapped the windows. Beyond them, the sea was black and restless. On the rocks below, a small boat was wedged between two waves, its mast broken.

Mara grabbed the emergency lantern and hurried down the stairs. But before she reached the door, the lighthouse spoke again.

“Wait.”

She froze. “There’s someone out there.”

“I know,” said the lighthouse. Its voice sounded like wind moving through an empty shell. “I have been waiting for them for a hundred years.”

In the lantern room, a brass panel she’d never noticed clicked open. Inside lay a letter, its paper yellow and brittle.

Mara unfolded it.

*To whoever tends this light: If my son comes home, tell him I kept the lamp burning.*

The letter was signed by the lighthouse’s first keeper, a woman named Elian.

Then, from somewhere below, came a knock.

Mara rushed to the entrance and pulled the door open. A young man stood in the rain, soaked and shaking. Behind him, the wrecked boat groaned against the rocks.

“I’m looking for Elian,” he gasped. “She was my great-great-grandmother. My family says she waited here for a sailor who never returned. I thought it was only a story.”

The lighthouse blazed to life.

Its beam swept over the water, over the rocks, over the young man’s astonished face. For one brief moment, in the bright glass, Mara saw an old woman standing beside her, smiling.

Then the light turned toward the sea again.

“Thank you,” whispered the lighthouse.

Mara looked at the young man. “Come in,” she said. “There’s someone you should meet.”

And for the first time in a hundred years, the lighthouse let the night grow quiet.
