### The Lighthouse Keeper’s Map

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs with a lantern in one hand and a cup of tea in the other. At the top, she turned the great light toward the sea.

Every night, the beam swept across black water, empty waves, and the same three stars.

Then, one Thursday, it found a ship.

The ship was small and battered, its sails hanging like torn sheets. No lights showed aboard. Mara watched it drift toward the rocks and grabbed the brass speaking horn.

“Turn north!” she shouted. “North, or you’ll break apart!”

The ship kept coming.

Mara spun the lighthouse beam toward the safer channel. Still, the vessel sailed straight ahead.

She hurried down the stairs and ran across the shore, waving her lantern. At the water’s edge, she saw a figure at the ship’s bow: a boy, soaked to the bone, holding a map.

He looked up at her.

“It’s no use!” he called. “The map says the harbor is here!”

Mara glanced at the map. It showed the coastline exactly as it had appeared a hundred years earlier, before storms had shifted the sandbars and swallowed the old harbor.

“Maps can grow old,” she called back. “Watch the light instead!”

The boy looked from the map to the lighthouse. For a moment, he hesitated. Then he turned the wheel.

The ship curved toward the channel, slipping past the rocks just as a wave rose behind it.

At dawn, the boy came ashore carrying the old map. He gave it to Mara.

“Keep it,” he said. “Maybe someone will need to know where the harbor used to be.”

Mara pinned it beside the lighthouse window, next to the newer charts.

After that, whenever ships came in from the dark, she kept both the old map and the light ready. One showed where people had been. The other showed where they could go.
