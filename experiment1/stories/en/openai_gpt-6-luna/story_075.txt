### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs and lit the lamp.

Every night, the beam swept across the black sea, past the rocks, past the empty harbor, and back again.

“Nothing out there,” she told the lighthouse.

The lighthouse never answered. It had stood on the cliff for a hundred years, and it had learned that people often said things they didn’t mean.

One winter evening, a storm rolled in so quickly that the gulls vanished before sunset. Mara lit the lamp. The beam swung out—and caught a tiny flash far beyond the rocks.

She leaned toward the glass.

Again: a flash. Then darkness.

Someone was signaling.

Mara grabbed the old brass horn and sounded it three times. The storm swallowed the call. She ran downstairs, unhooked the harbor bell, and struck it with all her strength.

Once. Twice. Three times.

From the sea came a faint answering clang.

Mara rushed back up and turned the lamp toward the signal. She held the beam steady, though the wind made the tower groan and the glass shudder in its frame.

Hours passed. At last, a small fishing boat appeared between the waves, guided toward the harbor by the light. Two sailors climbed onto the rocks, soaked and shaking. One carried a child wrapped in a yellow blanket.

The sailors thanked Mara. The child, still half asleep, reached for her hand.

“You kept the light on,” the child whispered.

Mara looked up at the great lamp. For the first time, she wondered what the lighthouse might say if it could speak.

Then the light swept across the sea again, patient as a heartbeat.

And Mara understood. The lighthouse had remembered every ship it had ever guided home.

It had simply been waiting for her to notice.
