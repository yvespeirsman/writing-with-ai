### The Lighthouse Keeper’s Library

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs with a book tucked beneath her arm.

She read at the top while the lamp turned slowly around her, sweeping its bright eye across the sea. The books came from a little library in town, and every week she returned one and borrowed another. She liked stories about distant places, though she had never traveled farther than the harbor.

One stormy evening, the lighthouse beam caught something unusual: a small boat drifting beyond the rocks. Mara rang the bell and ran down the stairs, calling for help. By the time the fishermen reached the water, the boat had vanished.

The next morning, Mara found a book on the lighthouse steps. Its cover was swollen with seawater. Inside, on the first page, someone had written:

*To the keeper of the light: I was lost. Thank you for showing me the way.*

Mara brought the book to the library. The librarian turned it over in her hands.

“We don’t own this one,” she said.

That night, Mara read it at the top of the lighthouse. It told the story of a sailor who had been lost at sea for many days, searching for a light to guide him home. The final pages were blank.

As the lamp turned, a voice rose from the waves.

“Keep reading,” it called.

Mara looked out. Far beyond the rocks, a boat bobbed in the darkness.

She opened the book and began to tell the sailor what happened next: how he found his way to shore, how he met a girl who kept a lighthouse, how she showed him the town library and all the stories he had missed while he was at sea.

The boat turned toward the harbor.

Mara read until dawn. By then, the blank pages had filled themselves with words.

Years later, when the old lighthouse became a museum, visitors were told that the keeper had owned a magical book. But Mara knew better.

The book wasn’t magic.

The light was.
