**The Last Light in the Harbor**

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs and lit the lamp.

She had done it for forty years, even after the harbor emptied, even after the fishing boats stopped coming, even after the town built a road that led travelers safely around the cliffs.

“Who are you guiding?” people asked.

Mara never answered.

Then, one winter night, a storm rose from the sea. Wind shook the windows. Waves climbed over the rocks. As Mara reached the top of the lighthouse, she saw a small boat beyond the reef, its sail torn and its lantern dark.

She lit the lamp.

Once. Twice. Again.

The boat turned toward the harbor.

By dawn, a young sailor stood at her door, shivering beneath a blanket.

“My grandmother told me,” he said, “if I ever lost my way, to look for the light that never gave up.”

Mara looked past him at the sea. For the first time in years, she smiled.

That night, when she climbed the stairs, she carried two cups of tea. She had a feeling she wouldn’t be keeping the light alone anymore.
