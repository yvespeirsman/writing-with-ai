### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, Mara climbed the cliff to light the old lighthouse.

She didn’t need to. The harbor had electric beacons now, bright enough to guide every boat safely home. But the lighthouse stood above the town like a watchful old friend, and Mara’s grandfather had kept its lamp burning until the night he disappeared.

So she kept it burning for him.

One stormy evening, as rain rattled the glass, Mara found a brass key on the floor. She’d swept the lighthouse a hundred times and had never seen it before. Its bow was shaped like a tiny star.

The key fit a narrow door beneath the stairs.

Behind it lay a spiral staircase descending into the cliff. Mara took a lantern and followed it down, past damp stone and the distant thunder of the sea. At the bottom, she found a round room with a window facing the water. On the wall hung dozens of little brass tags, each engraved with a name.

Her grandfather’s was there.

Beneath it, someone had scratched a message:

**For the one who keeps the light: remember that a lighthouse doesn’t call ships to itself. It helps them find their own way.**

Mara touched the tag. Somewhere beyond the glass, a ship’s horn sounded.

She looked out and saw a small fishing boat pitching in the waves, its lights lost in the storm. The old lighthouse lamp was glowing above her, but the storm swallowed its beam.

Mara ran up the stairs. At the top, she turned the great lens by hand, though it hadn’t moved in years. It groaned, then caught the light. A golden beam swept over the sea.

Once. Twice.

The boat turned toward the harbor.

Mara watched until it passed the breakwater. Then, in the brightening dawn, she saw someone standing on the deck, waving.

For a moment, she thought it was her grandfather.

But it was only a sailor, safe at last.

Mara smiled anyway. The light had never been meant for the dead. It was for everyone still finding their way home.
