### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse swept its beam across the sea.

Every night, the sea sent it back empty.

Mara had been the lighthouse keeper for six months, and she was beginning to think the old tower was lonely. It creaked in the wind. Its windows rattled during storms. Sometimes, just before dawn, its lamp flickered even though the machinery was sound.

“Lighthouses don’t get lonely,” her brother told her over the radio.

“Then why does this one keep looking for something?”

Her brother laughed. The radio crackled, and the conversation disappeared into static.

That night, a storm rolled in without warning. Rain battered the windows. The wind howled around the tower. Mara climbed the spiral stairs to check the lamp—and found it dark.

She rushed to the control panel. Everything was working. The fuel was full. The glass was clean. Still, the beam refused to turn.

Then she heard a knock.

Not at the door below, but at the window beside her.

Mara turned. Outside, in the middle of the storm, a small boat bobbed beside the tower. A girl stood in it, gripping the rail.

Mara ran down and flung open the door. The girl stumbled inside, soaked and shivering.

“I followed the light,” she said.

“But the light’s out.”

The girl looked up at the tower. “Not anymore.”

Above them, the lamp burst into life. Its beam swept across the sea, brighter than Mara had ever seen it. Far off, a ship answered with three long blasts from its horn.

By morning, the storm had passed. The girl was gone. No footprints crossed the wet floor. No boat floated beneath the cliffs.

But on the windowsill lay a small silver button, warm to the touch.

After that, the lighthouse never flickered again.

And on the loneliest nights, when the sea was black and empty, Mara would see a tiny light moving far beyond the reef—coming and going, as if someone out there were looking back.
