### The Lighthouse That Remembered

Every night, the lighthouse swept its beam across the sea.

Every night, the sea sent it back empty.

Mara had lived on the island since she was born. Her father had tended the lighthouse before her, and his father before him. They all said the same thing: keep the lamp lit, even when no ships are expected. Especially then.

“Someone may be lost,” her father used to say.

Then, one winter evening, he sailed out to repair a buoy and never returned.

Mara kept the lamp lit.

Years passed. The paint peeled from the tower. The village thinned as people moved to the mainland. Still, every night, Mara climbed the spiral stairs and turned the great brass light toward the dark.

Then, on a night of hard rain, the beam caught something on the rocks below.

A boy stood there, soaked and shivering, holding a small wooden boat. Mara hurried down to him.

“Where did you come from?” she asked.

The boy pointed out to sea. “I was looking for the way home.”

Mara wrapped him in her coat and brought him inside. She gave him tea, a blanket, and a place by the fire. But when she asked his name, he only stared at the lighthouse window.

“It called me,” he said.

In the morning, the storm had passed. Mara went to the shore with him, but there were no footprints in the sand—only the small wooden boat, neatly set beside the rocks.

The boy was gone.

That evening, as Mara climbed the stairs, she noticed something carved into the brass railing. The letters were old and familiar, scratched there by a hand she remembered:

**Keep the lamp lit. I’m still finding my way.**

For the first time in years, Mara smiled.

She turned the great light toward the sea.

And far beyond the dark water, another light answered.
