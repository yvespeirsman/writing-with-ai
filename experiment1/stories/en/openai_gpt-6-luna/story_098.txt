### The Lighthouse Keeper’s Map

Every night, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs with a lantern in one hand and a cup of tea in the other. She lit the great lamp, watched its beam sweep across the sea, and waited for the ships to answer with their tiny lights.

One stormy evening, she found a map tucked beneath the lantern’s brass base. It showed the coastline, the lighthouse, and a small island that didn’t appear on any chart. A red X marked its center.

Mara studied the map until dawn. Then, when the weather cleared, she borrowed a fishing boat and sailed toward the empty horizon.

The island appeared at sunset.

It was no more than a circle of black rock, with a single tree growing from its center. Beneath the tree sat a wooden box. Inside was a stack of letters—hundreds of them, all addressed to the lighthouse keeper.

The oldest was signed by Mara’s grandfather, who had vanished at sea before she was born.

*If you’ve found this,* he had written, *then the light is still burning. I used to think a lighthouse was meant to guide ships home. I was wrong. It guides the lonely, too.*

Mara read the letter twice. Then she looked out at the water, where evening had begun to gather.

Back at the lighthouse, she lit the lamp.

Far beyond the waves, a tiny light answered.  
Then another.  
Then another.
