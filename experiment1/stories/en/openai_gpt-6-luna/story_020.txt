### The Lighthouse Keeper’s Last Map

Every night, Mara climbed the spiral stairs of the lighthouse and lit the lamp.

Every night, the beam swept across the sea, past the black rocks and the sleeping harbor, until it reached the far horizon.

And every night, Mara wondered what lay beyond it.

Her grandfather had been the lighthouse keeper before her. When he died, she found his old maps in a drawer—maps of the coast, the islands, and the deep channels where ships could vanish. But one map was different. It showed no land at all. Only a single line drawn from the lighthouse into the empty sea.

At its end, her grandfather had written: *For the one who is brave enough to follow.*

One moonless night, the lighthouse lamp went dark.

Mara hurried to the top and found the great glass lens cracked. The wind howled through the room. Below, she heard the warning bell from the harbor: a ship was coming in through the fog.

There was no time to repair the lamp.

Mara grabbed the strange map, a lantern, and her grandfather’s little wooden boat. She rowed into the darkness, following the line on the paper. The sea grew rough, and soon the lighthouse disappeared behind her.

Then, far ahead, a light blinked once.

Mara rowed toward it.

A ship emerged from the fog, its sails torn and its deck crowded with frightened passengers. Mara raised her lantern and waved it above her head. The captain spotted her and turned the ship toward the safe channel.

When the vessel reached harbor, its grateful crew offered Mara gold, warm clothes, even a place aboard their ship.

She refused them all.

“Did your grandfather draw that map?” the captain asked.

Mara looked down. The ink had vanished. In its place was a new line, leading from the harbor toward a distant island.

“I think he left it for me,” she said.

The captain smiled. “Then you’d better find out where it goes.”

At dawn, Mara returned to the lighthouse. She repaired the lamp, then packed her maps and stepped aboard the waiting ship.

For the first time in her life, she sailed toward the horizon—not to see what lay beyond it, but to find out.
