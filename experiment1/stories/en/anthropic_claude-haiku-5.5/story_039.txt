**The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Letter**

For forty-one years, Eleanor Vance had climbed the one hundred and twelve iron steps of the Harrow Point lighthouse each evening to light the lamp. She knew every rivet in the spiral staircase, every creak in the third landing, every gull that perched on the railing like it owned the place.

The town below had changed around her. The cannery closed. The young people left for the cities. The fishing boats thinned out until only a handful remained, bobbing like tired thoughts in the harbor. But the light kept turning, and Eleanor kept turning it on, because someone had to.

One October evening, a letter arrived by the supply boat. The envelope was thin, the handwriting unfamiliar, the return address a hospital in Portland.

*Dear Ms. Vance,*

*You don't know me. My name is Thomas Reed. Thirty years ago, your light guided my father's boat home through a storm that took three other men. He told me about it before he died. He said that light was the only thing he could see, and that he prayed to it the whole night.*

*I wanted you to know that it mattered. I'm not sure why I'm writing now. Maybe because I'm dying, and it seems like the kind of thing a person should say out loud before the chance is gone.*

*Thank you for keeping the light.*

*Thomas*

Eleanor read it three times. Then she folded it carefully and placed it in the pocket of her wool coat.

That night, she climbed the one hundred and twelve steps slower than usual. At the top, she lit the lamp and watched its beam sweep across the black water, reaching for boats she could not see.

She had always thought of the work as lonely. Tonight, for the first time, it felt like a conversation with people she would never meet.

She stayed at the window until dawn, just in case someone else was out there, needing to find their way home.
