**The Last Lighthouse Keeper**

For forty-one years, Eleanor Vance climbed the spiral staircase of the Gannet Point lighthouse every evening at dusk. She wound the great clockwork mechanism, trimmed the wick, and watched the lamp sweep its golden arm across the black Atlantic. Ships rarely passed anymore. Automated buoys and satellites had made her work a relic, but Eleanor kept the light burning all the same.

The letter from the coast guard arrived in October. The station was closing. The lighthouse would be decommissioned by the new year, its lens removed and shipped to a museum in Boston.

She read it twice at the kitchen table, then folded it into her apron pocket and climbed the tower as the sun bled into the horizon.

That night, something unexpected happened. Just after nine, a small fishing boat appeared out of the fog, its single lantern wavering. Eleanor watched through the glass as it drifted toward the rocks, then corrected course, steering safely past the reef and into the harbor below.

The next morning, a young man knocked on her door. His name was Theo, and he'd been the boat's only passenger, a sailor's son coming home after his father's funeral.

"My dad used to talk about this light," he said. "Said it was the only thing that ever brought him home."

Eleanor invited him in for tea. They talked for hours, about the sea, about fathers, about the strange ways people find their way back to places they love.

When Theo left, he promised to visit before the lens was taken down. Eleanor found herself smiling as she climbed the tower that evening, winding the clock one final time with steady hands.

The light still had work to do, and so did she.
