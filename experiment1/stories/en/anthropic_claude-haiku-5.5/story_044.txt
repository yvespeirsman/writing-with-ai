**The Last Lighthouse Keeper**

The fog rolled in on a Tuesday, thick and gray, the way it always did in October. Marguerite Vale climbed the spiral stairs of the lighthouse at Gannet's Point one final time, her knees complaining with every step, and paused at the top to catch her breath.

Below her, the Atlantic churned against the rocks. Above her, the great lamp sat dark. It had been dark for six weeks, ever since the automated system was installed and the Coast Guard had sent the letter thanking her for forty-one years of service.

She had not been able to bring herself to leave.

"Foolish," she muttered, and then she laughed at herself, because who else was there to hear it?

Her husband, Thomas, had kept this light with her for the first nine years before his heart gave out on a winter morning. After that, it had been only her. She had polished the Fresnel lens every week, trimmed the wicks, wound the clockwork mechanism that turned the beam, and logged every ship that passed in the careful copperplate hand Thomas had taught her.

*Visibility poor. Fog bank east. Lamp lit at 18:42. All quiet.*

Tonight, she decided, she would light it one last time by hand.

It took her a while to find the matches in the drawer where she'd left them, and longer still to remember the exact sequence: the valve, the mantle, the slow turn of the brass key. When the flame caught, it bloomed gold behind the glass, and she felt something in her chest loosen.

She stood by the rail and watched the beam sweep across the water, out through the fog, reaching for ships she could no longer see.

Far to the east, a single running light blinked back at her. Then another. A small fishing boat, feeling its way home.

Marguerite smiled and lifted a hand, though no one could see her.

"Come on, then," she whispered. "I've got you."

She stayed until the boat's light turned into the harbor mouth and disappeared. Then she descended the stairs for the last time, leaving the lamp burning behind her, a light that no longer needed a keeper but kept shining anyway.
