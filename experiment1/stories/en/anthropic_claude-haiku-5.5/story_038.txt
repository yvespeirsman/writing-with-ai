**The Last Lighthouse Keeper's Letter**

The fog came in on a Tuesday, the way it always did in October, thick and silver, swallowing the harbor one boat at a time.

Marta Hollis climbed the one hundred and twelve steps of the Gannet Point lighthouse for the last time just before dusk. Her knees complained with every turn of the spiral stair. The automated system would take over at the end of the month, and the town council had been very polite about it, the way people are polite at funerals.

At the top, she lit the lamp by hand, as she had every night for thirty-one years. The great lens caught the flame and threw it out across the water in slow, patient sweeps.

Her father had kept this light before her. He used to say a lighthouse wasn't really for the ships. "Ships have their own eyes now," he'd said, tapping his radar set with a grease-stained finger. "The light is for the people who are scared to be out there alone. It says: someone is still awake. Someone is still watching."

She had never quite believed him. Tonight, she thought he might have been right.

Below her, a single set of headlights appeared on the coast road, wavering through the mist. Then they stopped. A car door opened. A figure stood at the edge of the cliff, looking up at the beam.

Marta watched for a long while. The figure didn't move. Then, slowly, they raised one arm and waved.

She had no idea who it was. A fisherman's widow, maybe, or a teenager who'd driven out to think, or someone who simply needed to see that the light was still burning before they could sleep.

Marta lifted her hand and waved back.

She stayed up there until the sky went from black to gray, tending the flame, making sure the light kept sweeping across the water, telling the dark that someone was still awake.
