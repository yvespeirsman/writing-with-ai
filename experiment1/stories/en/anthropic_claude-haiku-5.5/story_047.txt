**The Last Lighthouse Keeper**

For forty-one years, Eleanor Vance had climbed the one hundred and twelve steps of the Gull Point lighthouse every evening at dusk, wound the great brass clockwork, and lit the lamp that guided ships past the jagged rocks below. When the automated system was installed in the spring of 1987, the town council sent a polite letter thanking her for her service and suggesting she might enjoy her retirement.

She did not enjoy it. She sat in her cottage by the water and listened to the foghorn that no longer needed her, and she felt the silence in her chest like a missed step.

One stormy October night, the new light failed. Eleanor saw it from her window, the beam stuttering and then going dark. Within the hour, the harbor radio crackled with alarm. A fishing boat, the *Marguerite*, was somewhere out past the point, its captain calling for a bearing he could not find in the rain.

Eleanor did not hesitate. She pulled on her oilskins, took her old lantern from its hook by the door, and walked through the wind to the point. Her knees ached. The path was slick. But she knew every stone of it.

At the base of the tower, the maintenance door was locked. She had kept the spare key on a nail inside her cottage for four decades, out of habit, and she had brought it with her without quite knowing why.

Inside, the machinery hummed uselessly. She found the fault in minutes, a fuse blown by water seeping through an old seal. She had replaced a hundred such fuses in her day. Her hands remembered before her mind did.

The lamp flared back to life. Its beam swept across the black water, and somewhere out there, a voice on the radio cried out in relief.

Eleanor climbed the stairs slowly, one hundred and twelve steps, and sat on the top landing as the light turned above her, steady and bright, the way it always had.

When the *Marguerite* finally limped into harbor at dawn, its crew found a small, weathered woman asleep on a bench beside the lighthouse, her lantern still burning in her lap.
