**The Last Lighthouse Keeper**

For forty-one years, Eleanor Vance climbed the spiral stairs of the Gull Point lighthouse every evening at dusk. She climbed them when her knees ached, when the winter winds rattled the glass, and once, memorably, with a broken wrist wrapped in a dish towel.

The light had been automated a decade earlier, so there was no real need for her anymore. The Coast Guard had sent a polite letter thanking her for her service. The town had thrown a small party with sheet cake. Her son in Denver had called to say she should finally come live near them.

Still, every evening, she climbed.

On the night before she was to move inland, Eleanor reached the top of the tower and found the lamp already glowing. She stood still, her hand on the rail, uncertain. The machinery hummed on its own, the great lens turning slowly, sweeping its beam across the dark water as it had for over a century.

She sat down on the iron floor, her back against the cold brass housing, and watched the light cross the waves. Out past the rocks, a fishing boat's small lantern bobbed toward the harbor. Its captain, she knew, would be watching for this light the way she had once watched for his father's.

*Someone still needs to see it,* she thought.

She didn't need to tend the lamp anymore. But she understood, sitting there in the hum and the turning glow, that a lighthouse was never really about the machinery. It was about the promise that someone was watching the dark on behalf of those who were out in it.

The next morning, she packed her boxes and left the keys on the kitchen table, along with a note for whoever came next:

*The light doesn't need you. But it's glad of company.*

Then she drove inland, and did not look back, because she had already said everything the tower had ever asked of her.
