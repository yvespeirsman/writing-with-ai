**The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Letter**

For forty-one years, Eleanor Vance had climbed the one hundred and twelve steps of the Harrow Point lighthouse each evening, lit the great lamp, and watched the sea swallow the sun. Her husband, Thomas, had kept the light before her, and before him, his father. Now the light was hers alone, and she tended it the way one tends a grave: faithfully, without expecting anything back.

The automated system had been installed in the spring. A men in a white van had come with crates of humming equipment, shaken her hand politely, and told her she was welcome to stay in the cottage as long as she liked. The Coast Guard was grateful, they said. Times had changed.

Eleanor had nodded and said nothing. She had polished the brass one last time that night, and climbed the steps slowly, her knees complaining at every turn.

At the top, the lens caught the last of the orange light and scattered it across the water. She stood there longer than she needed to.

In the drawer of the small desk below, beneath her husband's old logbooks, was a letter she had started writing to Thomas three winters ago, after the fever took him. She had never finished it. Tonight, she took it out, unfolded it, and read the first line aloud to the empty room:

*"The light is still burning, my love. I wanted you to know that I kept it going until the end."*

She folded the letter again, tucked it into her coat pocket, and went down the steps for the last time.

Behind her, the lighthouse turned on, sweeping its patient beam across the dark water, as it had for a hundred and sixty years. No one was there to see it but the gulls, and the gulls did not mind.
