**The Last Lighthouse Keeper**

For forty-one years, Elena Marsh climbed the spiral stairs of the Gannet Point lighthouse every evening at dusk. She counted the steps out of habit: one hundred and twelve, then the iron ladder, then the lamp room with its great lens turning like a slow glass heart.

When the automated system came in, the town council sent a letter. Her position would be "retired with gratitude." The lighthouse would be dark by spring.

Elena read the letter twice, folded it into quarters, and put it in the drawer with her mother's sewing scissors. Then she went up the stairs anyway.

No ships needed her now. The radar and satellites guided them, and the captains who passed in the night would never know her name. But she still polished the brass. She still trimmed the wick of the backup lamp and checked the oil. It was a habit, and habits were the only thing that had ever kept her steady.

In October, a fog rolled in thick enough to swallow the harbor. The automated system flickered during a storm, and the foghorn died with a cough. Elena heard it from her kitchen and stood very still.

She grabbed her coat and ran.

By the time she reached the tower, her lungs burned and her hands shook on the railing. She climbed the hundred and twelve steps in the dark, counting under her breath, and lit the old lamp by hand. The beam swept out over the water, pale and wavering, cutting through the fog.

Far out in the channel, a fishing boat turned its bow toward the light.

Then another. Then a freighter's horn answered, long and low, and Elena pressed her palm to the cold glass and whispered, "I see you."

She stayed up all night, feeding the lamp, watching the small lights of the fleet come home one by one.

In the morning, the council sent a second letter. It was shorter than the first, and it said only: *Please stay.*

Elena smiled, hung her coat by the door, and climbed the stairs.
